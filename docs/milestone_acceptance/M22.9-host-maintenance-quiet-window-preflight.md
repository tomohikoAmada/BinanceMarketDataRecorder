## Quiet-window procedure and test

The accepted mechanism is deliberately narrow and reversible:

1. Complete all pending package work before any Recorder pre-start gate or T0.
2. Stop `apt-daily.timer`, `apt-daily-upgrade.timer`, their two services, and
   `unattended-upgrades.service`.
3. Apply `systemctl mask --runtime` to those five units. Runtime masks prevent
   activation during the measurement window, do not change the persistent
   enablement policy, and disappear on reboot.
4. Require all five units to be `masked-runtime` and inactive; require no
   apt/dpkg worker or lock holder.
5. Treat a successful start of any masked maintenance service as a failed
   pre-start gate. The preflight's explicit `systemctl start apt-daily.service`
   probe was rejected with exit status 1 because the unit was masked.
6. During the bounded 60-second test hold, all five units remained inactive,
   the boot ID and PID 1 start identity remained stable, Recorder remained
   inactive and disabled, and no systemd reexecution was observed.
7. Before handoff, remove only the runtime masks and restore/start the two apt
   timers and `unattended-upgrades.service`; verify their persistent enabled
   and current active states.

The future Formal 2-hour retry must repeat steps 1–5 immediately before its
fresh identity/readiness/capacity gates, keep the runtime masks for the whole
measurement window, and execute step 7 on every controlled exit. A reboot is
an emergency fail-closed exit because it clears runtime masks but also
invalidates the Formal process/boot identity and awards zero credit.
