## Delivered scope

- Additive Catalog archive transaction/event tables and synchronized chunk
  states from `ARCHIVE_COPYING` through `LOCAL_DELETED`.
- Oldest-sealed deterministic reservation, bounded streaming copy to an exact
  transaction-owned `.copying` path, file/directory fsync, reopen/full readback,
  stored-size/SHA-256 verification, atomic rename, and external manifest.
- Revalidation of volume UUID, registered relative path, storage ID and marker
  nonce before every external phase. Only `raw/` and `manifests/` below the
  registered directory are created.
- Collision-safe idempotence: a byte-identical final file/manifest is reused
  after full validation; conflicting user data is never overwritten. Only the
  current transaction's exact residual temporary file is cleaned.
- Verified external Catalog commit followed by separately authorized,
  revalidated and committed local deletion. Internal Raw manifest provenance is
  retained, and unique-copy warnings are emitted.
- Idempotent archive/delete metrics, completed-archive backlog subtraction, and
  structured `archive status`, `archive retry [--storage-id]`, and
  `archive verify <storage-id>` commands.
- Explicit `DISAPPEARED_DURING_COPY` evidence while the Collector remains
  outside the archive dependency path.

## Known limitations

M10 exposes one-item manual retry rather than a continuously supervised archive
worker; service scheduling belongs to the later supervisor/launchd milestones.
External verification proves byte identity and transaction durability, not
independent backup redundancy. After local deletion, the external artifact may
be the only Raw copy. Physical filesystem durability/removal behavior remains
an M17 evidence item. Internal Raw manifests are intentionally retained as
small immutable provenance records after the corresponding event artifact is
deleted.

## Data compatibility and rollback

M10 does not alter EventEnvelope or Raw chunk bytes. It additively extends the
Catalog and operational counters and creates versioned external archive
manifests. Rollback stops new archive allocation, reconciles in-flight rows,
and never removes external data or an internal source lacking verified deletion
authorization.
