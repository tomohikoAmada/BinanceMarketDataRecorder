## Result and cause

Fourteen canonical sample files and the 223,145,439-byte final record were
published. Final elapsed BOOTTIME is `7677610836692` ns, above the required
`7200000000000` ns. Duration alone therefore completed, but eligibility did
not: `sample-00000005.json` first recorded
`acceptance_observation_gap`, and the largest observed sample gap reached
`696.438246769` seconds. Blocking findings are monotonic, so the canonical
final retained that blocker and correctly finalized as `INCOMPLETE`.

The Recorder itself remained active with PID `35683`, InvocationID
`cd5d1dcc7ce54c10a15c166c893af609`, service instance
`4829aec5-4a34-41cd-94ef-bb972c31446c`, boot ID unchanged, and
`NRestarts=0`. The failure is therefore in the acceptance evidence path, not a
demonstrated four-ProductKey Recorder continuity failure.

The evidence-growth mechanism is concrete: each observation embeds the full
Catalog integrity result, reconnect continuation/inventory, and manifest
inventory. Later samples and the final are approximately 223 MB each. Audit,
serialization, durable publication, and the next full scan eventually take
longer than the 600-second cadence. The installed completed-stage verifier also
materializes every full sample document in one list, so it is not safe to run
against this multi-gigabyte chain on the 4 GiB VPS.

The next engineering milestone is deliberately narrow: bound repeated observer
evidence and make completed-stage verification streaming/bounded-memory while
preserving immutable hashes, exact chain authority, lifecycle integrity, and
fail-closed eligibility. Repeating the unchanged live run is not eligible.

zero. This happened after the first observation-gap blocker and therefore did
not cause the original failure, but it independently confirms that future
review and verification must stream bounded fields rather than materialize the
full evidence chain.
