## Startup-liveness incident

The candidate was deployed with exact static identity and passed fresh-venv,
hashed dependency, Wheel installation, `pip check`, `direct_url.json`, and
systemd static verification. It started as PID `328413` with InvocationID
`59829e72ffab41bf85047b9a9de12b15`, but readiness failed with stale heartbeat;
the service remained `STARTING`, recovery was incomplete, and capacity and
markets were unavailable.

Forensic verdict: `ROOT_CAUSE_CONFIRMED_LONG_RECOVERY_HEARTBEAT_GAP`. Both old
and candidate source shared the behavior. The retained production corpus had
66,354 sealed and unarchived chunks, 12,331,419,711 compressed Raw bytes,
196,899,977,168 logical uncompressed bytes, 98,722,391 manifest bytes, and an
85,217,280-byte Catalog with zero WAL bytes. Recovery historically took about
17m50s and performed 64,174 actions while fully revalidating at least
221,661,538,981 bytes of stable sealed data. One `STARTING` heartbeat was
published before synchronous `recover_storage()` ran in `asyncio.to_thread()`;
the 5-second heartbeat and 30-second stale threshold expired long before
healthy recovery completed. No artifact pathology, Catalog/filesystem fault,
OOM, clock fault, or recovery hang was identified.

The same defect had a shutdown-liveness aspect: after SIGTERM during recovery,
the outstanding recovery worker did not cooperatively stop, so the process
exceeded `TimeoutStopSec=90s` and systemd used SIGKILL. This was application
level outstanding-worker behavior, not Linux D-state or kernel-uninterruptible
I/O.

The final production host state after rollback was service failed/stopped,
`MainPID=0`, zero production writers, zero active partials, and old deployment
authority restored. The old runtime exhibited the same readiness failure, so
the failure was not specific to the candidate artifact. The service must not
be described as currently recording.

## Startup correction status

The correction keeps a single periodic heartbeat active during recovery,
retains `STARTING`/not-ready semantics, keeps collectors after recovery, uses
metadata-only reconciliation only for exact stable `SEALED` Catalog+manifest
identity, preserves full validation for crash-unstable states, and adds
cooperative recovery shutdown during `recover_storage()`. Local results were focused `97 passed`, full
suite `1361 passed, 24 skipped, 4 deselected`, with Ruff, MyPy, M0 contracts,
and Go Raw golden verification passing. The targeted review proved the single
heartbeat owner, stable-sealed fast path safety and authority, crash-unstable
full validation, cancel/resume idempotence, no new cross-thread Catalog race,
readiness semantics, and reduced startup cost. It judged 300 seconds
architecturally plausible but not production-timing-proven.

The positive findings do not make the feature ready for PR. A pre-existing P1
remains in `553cb345`: SIGTERM during the post-recovery `STARTING` capacity
observation can set `STOPPING`, then have the returning observation permit
`RUNNING`, `SERVICE_STARTED`, and Collector task creation. This violates the
no `STOPPING -> RUNNING` transition and no-Collector-start-after-stop contract.
The P1 was not introduced by `2e8525f`.

The review also recorded one P2, nonblocking for this architecture: a missing
or size-mismatched already-stable local `SEALED` artifact becomes a
`reconcile_failed` RecoveryAction rather than forcing startup failure. It is
pre-existing post-commit external-loss/filesystem-corruption behavior and is
not promoted to P1.

Cooperative stop during `recover_storage()` itself remains implemented and
tested, but the full startup-stop contract is not closed. No Catalog schema,
capacity policy, readiness threshold, or systemd timeout changed.

## Confirmed production defect

Eight Recorder exits shared the fatal chain
`IngressBackpressureTimeout -> IngressPostCloseHandoffTimeout ->
CoreMarketTerminalFailure`; five were USD-M `book_ticker`, one was Spot
`book_ticker`, and two additional USD-M `book_ticker` exits occurred during
the intended formal window.

The queue and fail-fast policies behaved as designed. The correctness defect
was the USD-M fatal post-close branch: after the received boundary frame failed
to enter the bounded writer handoff, `IngressPostCloseHandoffTimeout` escaped
while `UsdMStreamCollector.run()` still held `outcome="stopped"`. Its `finally`
block consequently built no reconnect seal intent, wrote no
`STREAM_DISCONTINUITY_STARTED`, applied no `reconnect_gap`, and allowed the
writer to seal the preceding Raw tail with ordinary completeness semantics
before the exception reached core supervision.

The boundary frame had been received from the WebSocket but was not admitted
to the writer queue and was not persisted. Its SHA-256 may be retained as
forensic boundary identity, but `boundary_frame_persisted=false`; the payload
is never inserted into Raw and never receives a fabricated EventEnvelope.

Spot contains the corresponding fatal-boundary guard at this authority. Its
previous test stopped after checking one incomplete manifest and STARTED row;
the correction strengthens Spot coverage through fresh startup recovery,
authentic first-new Raw sync, same-gap COMPLETED, and no payload fabrication.
USD-M now follows the same already-authoritative ADR-0027 ordering.

## First correction

On a USD-M backpressure-origin post-close handoff timeout the first correction
now:

1. retains the exact in-memory boundary identity and payload digest;
2. records that the handoff failed and remembers the closing connection;
3. classifies the fatal path as `ingress_backpressure` before writer release;
4. builds one versioned reconnect seal intent with
   `boundary_frame_persisted=false` and the exact gap identity;
5. durably writes STARTED before ordinary old-tail sealing, or preserves the
   same intent through the existing SEALING fallback if STARTED itself fails;
6. forces manifest-only `reconnect_gap`, so the old tail is
   `gap=true`/`complete=false` without mutating Raw bytes;
7. re-raises `IngressPostCloseHandoffTimeout`, preserving fail-fast core and
   systemd restart semantics.

Startup recovery remains the existing R1/R2/R2.1/R2.2 implementation. It
restores the same open `gap_id`; the first authentic replacement frame alone
receives `sequence_gap`; Raw sync precedes one exact COMPLETED event with
`historical_continuity_restored=false`.

No queue capacity, queue boundedness, saturation budget, post-close timeout,
Raw format, Catalog schema, reconnect reason taxonomy, or restart policy
changed.

## Independent-review follow-up correction

Independent review of corrective commit
`2610d7daef9f6371f020522fcfaceab137eea099` confirmed the production R-054
branch above, then found an adjacent P1 in both Spot and USD-M. The separate
post-close handoff after `IngressStopRequested` could raise the same
`IngressPostCloseHandoffTimeout`, but `run()` inferred
`ingress_backpressure` solely from the exception class. In addition,
`_backpressure_boundary_handoff_succeeded=True` could survive into a later
generation and suppress manifest forcing for that misclassified boundary.

All four post-close handoff call sites are now explicit. The backpressure sites
preserve `ingress_backpressure`; the stop-interrupted sites freeze whether the
actual authority is `session_restart` or true global stop before awaiting the
post-close put. A failed session-restart handoff retains the exact received
frame identity and connection/timestamp context with
`boundary_frame_persisted=false`, persists STARTED or the same SEALING intent,
forces `reconnect_gap`, and remains fatal. A failed global-stop handoff remains
fatal but seals with the existing shutdown semantics and creates no reconnect
gap because no replacement connection is authorized.

At every generation start the boundary frame, handoff result, post-close
outcome, connection identity, detected time, forced flags, seal intent, and
active connection identity are reset. Restored `_pending_gap` authority and
its first-new marker state are intentionally not erased. A generation-N
successful backpressure handoff therefore cannot authorize generation N+1.
