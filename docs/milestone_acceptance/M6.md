## Delivered scope

- Strict derived adapters for M4/M5 diff-depth, snapshot-provenance and
  bookTicker envelopes. Exact Raw payloads and sealed chunks are not changed.
- Required buffer-before-snapshot bootstrap for both markets.
- Current official Spot bridge/discard/live `U/u` rules and separate USD-M
  bridge/discard/`pu` continuity rules.
- Absolute price-level replacement, zero removal including absent levels,
  deterministic best bid/ask and canonical SHA-256 book hashes.
- Explicit `BUFFERING`, `SYNCHRONIZED`, and `RESYNC_REQUIRED` states. A gap
  makes the book unavailable to reliable consumers until a new snapshot bridge.
- Immutable unreliable intervals with last reliable ID, offending `U/u`,
  receive time, reason and resync end. `complete` remains false after resync.
- Empty-side, crossed-book, duplicate/stale, resync, and same-update-ID
  bookTicker audits. bookTicker is explicitly not called an exchange checksum.
- Atomic, fsynced `orderbook-checkpoint.v1` files with whole-document hash,
  logical book hash, source Raw chunk hashes, algorithm and collector versions,
  incomplete interval history, plus metadata-only Catalog registration.

## Architecture decisions

- ADR-0011 freezes the market-specific algorithms, deterministic decimal/book
  encoding, reliable-book boundary, audit semantics and checkpoint format.
- Spot and USD-M reconstruction inputs cannot be mixed. A deleted event is
  detected by `U > local+1` for Spot and `pu != previous u` for USD-M.
- Duplicate/stale updates already covered by local `u` are ignored because
  quantities are absolute; the next unseen event still faces the full official
  continuity rule.
- Checkpoints are derived and require source chunk SHA-256 lineage. SQLite does
  not store price levels or market-event payloads.
- Snapshot depth limits mean the result is a local bounded-snapshot-derived
  book, not a claim of every exchange price level.

## Excluded scope and known limitations

M6 does not implement M7 side data, daily metric persistence, normalized
Parquet, general replay APIs, archive operations, Collector integration/service
orchestration, trading/account behavior, strategies, backtests, GUI or web UI.

The reconstruction library consumes envelopes supplied by a caller; live
service wiring and persisted daily quality counters remain later milestones.
No new online smoke is required by M6 because sequence and deletion acceptance
must be deterministic; the three online tests were therefore not run. The
million-event M3 stress test was not rerun because M6 does not modify the Raw
writer or bounded ingress queue. Snapshot limits leave untouched out-of-range
levels unknown until a later update. Crossed/empty/ticker audits are evidence,
not an undocumented exchange checksum. Checkpoints are a derived optimization;
an otherwise valid checkpoint file left behind by a Catalog registration
failure is safe and rebuildable but is not yet automatically reconciled.

## Data compatibility and rollback

M6 does not alter `event-envelope.v1`, `raw-chunk.v1`, Raw manifests, Spot or
USD-M Collector wire capture. It adds derived `logical-orderbook.v1`,
`orderbook-checkpoint.v1`, `binance-local-orderbook.v1`, the
`data/checkpoints/` directory and a metadata-only Catalog table.

Rollback stops derived reconstruction, removes derived checkpoint/Catalog
metadata if desired, and reverts M6. All Raw remains immutable and sufficient
to rebuild using a corrected versioned algorithm.
